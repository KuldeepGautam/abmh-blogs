The difficulty, however, lies in recognising the difference between a panic attack and a heart attack, as both conditions can produce similar and alarming symptoms.

Chest tightness, breathlessness, dizziness, and sweating may occur in either condition. This uncertainty can delay medical evaluation, which is why awareness of symptom patterns is essential.

Understanding The Difference Between a Panic Attack and a Heart Attack

Although panic attacks originate from psychological stress and heart attacks result from reduced blood supply to the heart muscle, the symptoms may appear similar. Therefore, individuals experiencing chest discomfort often struggle to distinguish a panic attack from a heart attack event in real time.

A panic attack usually occurs when the body’s stress response is sudden. This reaction can produce rapid breathing, trembling, dizziness, and a feeling of impending danger. These signs of a panic attack may occur even when no immediate physical threat exists.

Conversely, a heart attack develops when the coronary arteries become partially or completely blocked. This blockage reduces oxygen supply to the heart muscle, leading to progressive heart attack symptoms such as heavy chest pressure, fatigue, nausea, and radiating pain.

Treatment And Management Approaches

Appropriate treatment depends on the underlying cause of symptoms. Therefore, clinicians focus first on determining whether the episode represents a panic attack vs heart attack.

Panic Attack Management

Once cardiac causes are excluded, doctors may recommend structured panic attack treatment. Management strategies commonly include:

  • Cognitive behavioural therapy to address anxiety triggers
  • Breathing exercises and relaxation techniques
  • Lifestyle adjustments to reduce chronic stress
  • Medication when clinically indicated

Learning how to handle panic attacks also plays an important role in long-term management. Patients benefit from recognising triggers, practising breathing control, and seeking psychological support when necessary.

Heart Attack Management

If evaluation confirms cardiac involvement, immediate medical intervention becomes essential. Treatment protocols may include:

  • Emergency medications to restore coronary blood flow
  • Advanced cardiac imaging and diagnostic tests
  • Coronary angiography and angioplasty, when required
  • Long-term cardiac rehabilitation and risk management
